Prove the guarantee holds on *your* corpus. `evaluate()` runs each row of a golden
CSV through `ask()` and returns an `EvaluationReport`.

<FacadeNote />

```python
from citenexus.evaluate import EvaluationReport

report: EvaluationReport = rag.evaluate("golden.csv")
print(report.total, report.answered, report.refused)
print(report.groundedness_rate, report.citation_rate, report.expected_support_rate)
```

## The golden CSV

The CSV needs a **`question`** column (`query` is also accepted) and an optional
**`expected`** column:

```csv
question,expected
"Can the employee disclose confidential information?","shall not disclose"
"What is the capital of France?",
```

- A row with an `expected` value counts as *expected-supported* when that text's
  content tokens are a subset of the answer's — a lightweight grounding check.
- A row with an **empty** `expected` counts as expected-supported **iff the row
  was answered** (`evaluate.py:76-77`).

<Aside type="danger" title="`evaluate()` cannot score abstention — and scores it backwards">
That second rule is the opposite of what you probably want. A blank `expected`
does **not** mean "this row should be refused". It means "any answer counts",
so a **correct refusal earns nothing and lowers `expected_support_rate`.**

This is why the [law example](/citenexus/benchmark-law/) reads 45% expected
support while its groundedness and citation rates are both 100% — five of its
eleven rows are refusals, four of them correct.

**Do not build a refusal regression gate on `expected_support_rate`.** To assert
abstention, drive `ask()` yourself and check the decision:

```python
from citenexus.answer.result import Decision

must_refuse = ["What is the maximum security deposit under this statute?"]
for q in must_refuse:
    assert rag.ask(q).evidence.decision is Decision.refused, q
```
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="`evaluate()` passes no options through to `ask()`">
`evaluate()` calls `ask(question)` bare — no `mode`, `k`, `answer_language` or
`search_languages` (`client.py:883-885`, `evaluate.py:72`). On a multilingual
corpus its counts will differ from a harness that fans out over
`search_languages`, and that difference is the missing fan-out, not a regression.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="What the report contains">
`EvaluationReport` carries counts — `total`, `answered`, `refused`, `grounded`
(answered rows where all claims verified), `cited` (answered rows with sources),
`expected_supported` — and derived rates: `groundedness_rate`, `citation_rate`,
`expected_support_rate`. It is returned **in memory**; `evaluate()` writes nothing
to disk or S3. Compute a refusal rate yourself as `report.refused / report.total`.
</Aside>
